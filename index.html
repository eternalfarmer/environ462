About the course

Most sustainability ideas don't fail because they're wrong. They fail because no one figured out who needs them, who will pay for them, and how to make them work. You can't solve a problem you haven't first understood from the people living it. This course is about that discovery, and about building solutions that are both profitable and sustainable, rather than just well-intentioned.

Your team will choose a problem, talk to the people affected, design a solution, test whether it works for people, planet and profit, and pitch it to experts.

Who it's for: students in any school or program, with no prerequisites.

How we work: as a collaboratory. We experiment, pitch often, and give each other constructive feedback.

Your team project

Create a profitable and sustainable solution

Your team will design a solution that creates real value for people and the planet, and can pay for itself. It doesn't have to be a venture-capital-backed startup. It can take many forms:

A startup

A new business built around a product or service that solves a sustainability problem.

A social enterprise

A mission-first business that reinvests in people and communities.

An intrapreneurship project

A new initiative, product line or process inside an existing company or organization.

A nonprofit or hybrid model

An organization that blends earned revenue with grants or donations.

A cooperative or community venture

Owned by and built for the people it serves, like the Amul dairy cooperative.

A public-sector or policy innovation

A new program, service or partnership with a city, agency or government.

Ways to fund it. Venture capital is one option, but there are many others:
Customer revenue / bootstrapping Grants & competition prizes Crowdfunding Impact investors Corporate partners Government programs Venture capital

Akshaya Kurian Varghese

Ph.D. Candidate in Strategy, Ross School of Business, University of Michigan · Former Deloitte Management Consultant

Leabo Teaching Award 2025–26 4.9 / 5.0 instructor rating

Education · Ph.D. Candidate, Michigan Ross · Ed.M., Harvard · B.B.A. & B.A., Michigan

Akshaya studies leading change and innovation, and entrepreneurship. Drawing on in-depth fieldwork, he examines these themes in a range of settings, including the automotive and construction industries, social enterprises, and design thinking firms.

Before his Ph.D., he spent six years at Deloitte Consulting in Boston as a management consultant, advising leadership teams on change and innovation at clients including Bank of America, American Express, Standard & Poor's, Cigna, Exelon, Johnson Controls, Land O'Lakes and the Commonwealth of Massachusetts. He also led Deloitte's pro bono work with College Bound Dorchester, a Boston nonprofit helping students get to and through college, which earned a Boston Business Journal Corporate Citizenship Award.

He then worked as a Research Associate at Harvard Business School, where he helped run one of the school's largest studies of innovation in senior teams, following five Fortune 500 companies working with a leading design firm. He has researched and written about organizations including Ford, Corning, Citadel, Kodak, Moleskine, Faber-Castell and Boston Ballet.

A first-generation college student, Akshaya was an undergraduate here at Michigan, where he lived in the Residential College and was a Resident Advisor in West Quad. He has now returned as a Ph.D. student.

For this course, he is writing an original case on how his grand-uncle, Verghese Kurien, built Amul, the world's largest dairy cooperative, and helped turn India from a milk-importing nation into the world's largest milk producer.
